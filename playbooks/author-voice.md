# Playbook — Author Voice System

## Use this when

Creating new articles, PR, scripts or social posts that should feel like a specific authorised author without copying their previous text.

## Sample selection

Use at least three samples across different topics or formats. Avoid selecting only the best-performing or most emotional pieces.

Record:

- date and platform;
- purpose and audience;
- topic;
- whether the author personally approved it;
- unusual constraints that may distort style.

## Analysis

### Observation position

What access, responsibility or lived experience shapes the author's view?

### Attention pattern

Which tensions do they repeatedly notice before others?

### Value order

When two goods conflict—speed versus depth, growth versus trust—what do they protect?

### Judgement method

Do they begin with a concrete scene, define a concept, compare incentives, examine evidence or challenge a common assumption?

### Reader relationship

Do they teach a peer, warn an operator, challenge an industry, persuade a client or accompany a learner?

### Emotional boundary

How much certainty, anger, humour, vulnerability or optimism is authentic?

## Produce the voice system

For each rule include:

```text
Rule:
Cross-sample evidence:
Confidence: high / medium / provisional
Effect on a new article:
What would violate the rule:
```

## Drafting a new topic

1. State the audience tension.
2. Ask what this author would notice first.
3. Decide what evidence they would trust.
4. Form the judgement before selecting a structure.
5. Choose an opening and sequence suited to this topic.
6. Remove borrowed catchphrases.

## Dual review

### Identity review

- Would the angle still feel authentic with different wording?
- Does the judgement follow the author's value order?
- Is the reader relationship correct?

### Originality review

- Is the structure newly chosen?
- Are examples and evidence specific to this topic?
- Is this more than an old article with nouns replaced?

Fail either review and revise.
