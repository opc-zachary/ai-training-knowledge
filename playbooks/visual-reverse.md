# Playbook — Visual Reverse Analysis

## Use this when

An authorised reference image communicates a desired visual direction more effectively than a verbal description.

## Step 1 — Define the intended use

Specify channel, aspect ratio, audience, message, product role and required visible text. Reverse analysis without a target becomes an exhaustive but unusable description.

## Step 2 — Separate transferable and non-transferable elements

Transferable:

- hierarchy;
- density and spacing rhythm;
- light behaviour;
- typography attitude;
- colour organisation;
- camera distance;
- commercial temperature;
- proof method.

Do not copy by default:

- brand identity;
- exact person;
- protected campaign copy;
- specific claim;
- category-inappropriate scene;
- proprietary illustration or composition.

## Step 3 — Structured analysis

### Layout

- canvas and ratio;
- grid and margins;
- dominant block;
- eye path;
- text/image balance;
- negative space.

### Typography

- headline scale and weight;
- support-copy density;
- alignment;
- contrast;
- emphasis devices;
- text safety zone.

### Visual system

- subject and camera;
- palette and contrast;
- background and material;
- light and shadow;
- texture and depth;
- repeated modules.

### Message proof

Cover the text and ask what the image still proves. If it proves nothing, the reference relies mainly on copy rather than visual evidence.

## Step 4 — Adaptation contract

Write:

```text
Keep from reference:
Rebuild for our product:
Do not copy:
Required factual elements:
Allowed invention:
Acceptance evidence:
```

## Step 5 — Generate and compare

Score candidate outputs on:

- message proof;
- product fidelity;
- visual DNA;
- category fit;
- text correctness;
- original distance from the reference.

Do not reward similarity alone.
