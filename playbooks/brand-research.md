# Playbook — Evidence-led Brand Research

## Use this when

Preparing for a client meeting, reviewing a brand's evolution, comparing competitors, defining positioning or building an annual communication direction.

## Phase 1 — Decision brief

Complete before research:

```text
Decision maker:
Decision to support:
Brand/company:
Market and geography:
Time range:
Products or business units:
Competitors and comparison logic:
Deadline:
```

If the decision is vague, the report will become a history essay.

## Phase 2 — Evidence plan

Prioritise:

1. official company filings, annual reports and product pages;
2. executive interviews and official announcements;
3. credible industry data with disclosed methodology;
4. campaign databases and trade publications;
5. platform discussion as perception evidence, not company fact.

For every claim record source, publisher, date, scope and access date.

## Phase 3 — Research structure

### Brand identity

- founding problem;
- target person and desired position;
- key assets and distinctive associations;
- current contradiction or challenge.

### Evolution

Divide history into 3–5 stages. For each stage capture:

- business condition;
- product or market move;
- brand concept and communication;
- representative campaign or channel;
- effect on perception;
- evidence and uncertainty.

### Portfolio

Compare major product lines by audience, use case, proposition, price logic, channel and communication method.

### Competitive perception

Separate company-intended positioning from audience perception. Compare mental availability, distinctiveness, trust, cultural relevance and price/value.

## Phase 4 — Contradiction review

Create a table for conflicting evidence:

| Claim | Source A | Source B | Likely reason | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Never average incompatible metrics without understanding the definitions.

## Phase 5 — Output

Deliver two layers:

- executive: decision, five findings, three risks, three actions;
- evidence: full narrative, tables, source ledger and uncertainty.

## Acceptance questions

1. Can a reviewer trace every decision-critical claim?
2. Are recent campaigns connected to business changes?
3. Are competitors comparable for a stated reason?
4. Does the recommendation follow from the evidence?
5. Is uncertainty visible rather than hidden in confident prose?
