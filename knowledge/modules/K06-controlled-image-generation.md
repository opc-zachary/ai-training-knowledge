# K06 — Controlled Image Generation

## Objective

Translate a visual requirement into controlled inputs, explicit change boundaries and verifiable output.

## Control methods

1. Reference-guided generation: borrow hierarchy, light or visual temperature.
2. Multi-image composition: assign layout, character, product and scene roles.
3. Local modification: change one selected element and preserve the rest.
4. Reverse analysis: extract typography, palette, layout and visual effects into structured guidance.

## Workflow

1. Define the image's business role.
2. Write what the image alone must prove.
3. Select references and assign each a role.
4. List exact product facts and protected elements.
5. Limit simultaneous changes.
6. Generate several candidates.
7. Validate product, people, text, claim and layout.
8. Regenerate failures with a corrected instruction.

## Acceptance

- Product geometry and colour are accurate.
- The image proves the intended message.
- All visible text is correct.
- No unsupported claim, price or certification appears.
- Human anatomy and interaction are plausible.
- The output fits the required channel and ratio.

## Failure signals

- “make it premium” is the main direction;
- a reference is copied rather than interpreted;
- too many changes cause identity drift;
- typography is accepted without character-level review;
- one attractive image has no role in the campaign.
