## Purpose

This video examines why AI writing often feels generic even when grammar is correct. The solution is not a longer list of adjectives; it is to provide business context, select an appropriate model, define a voice system and test whether the output preserves judgement rather than merely surface style.

## Author voice model

| Layer | Question | Bad shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Observation position | Where does the author stand? | Naming a profession only |
| Attention | What contradictions do they notice repeatedly? | Counting frequent nouns |
| Value order | What do they protect or reject? | Listing vague traits such as “sincere” |
| Judgement | How do they move from evidence to conclusion? | Copying sentence length |
| Reader relationship | Are they teaching, challenging, accompanying or selling? | Adding a friendly tone instruction |
| Emotional boundary | What feeling is permitted or avoided? | Forcing every article to be inspirational |
| Variable form | What structure can change by topic? | Reusing one viral template |

## Editorial workflow

1. Collect representative samples from different topics.
2. Identify cross-sample patterns and topic-specific accidents.
3. Write evidence for each claimed author trait.
4. Mark confidence as high, medium or provisional.
5. Convert each trait into a decision rule for a new topic.
6. Draft without copying an old structure.
7. Evaluate both “does it sound like this author?” and “is it more than a rewritten old article?”

## AI-writing quality checklist

- Does the opening identify a real tension?
- Is the main claim specific enough to disagree with?
- Does each paragraph add evidence, mechanism or consequence?
- Are abstract claims grounded in examples?
- Are transitions natural rather than mechanically symmetrical?
- Does the brand have a position, not just positive adjectives?
- Is the requested action appropriate to the reader relationship?
