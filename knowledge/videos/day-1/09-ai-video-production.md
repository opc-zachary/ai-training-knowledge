## Learning outcomes

- Understand how still-image planning supports video consistency.
- Evaluate video through usable shots, not only one impressive clip.
- Plan identity, scene, light, camera and motion continuity.
- Recognise where short clips, editing and selective human appearance reduce artefacts.
- Treat cost, generation duration and failure rate as production metrics.

## Video planning stack

| Layer | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Brief | Audience, message, duration, channel, call to action |
| Character bible | Identity, age, face, body, hair, clothing, emotional range |
| Scene bible | Location, time, light, palette, props, continuity rules |
| Shot list | Framing, camera movement, subject movement, start/end state |
| Generation | Model, reference inputs, duration, seed/variation strategy |
| Review | Identity, physics, anatomy, product, text, continuity, policy |
| Edit | Select, trim, order, sound, captions and final ratio |

## Usable-yield calculation

Do not budget by “price per generated second” alone.

```text
effective cost per usable second
= total generation spend
÷ seconds that survive review and edit
```

Track:

- attempted clips;
- technically valid clips;
- brand-valid clips;
- seconds used in final edit;
- human correction time;
- regeneration reason.

## Common artefacts

- face or clothing changes between angles;
- hands, eyes or teeth deform during motion;
- product geometry changes;
- background objects appear or disappear;
- light direction changes across cuts;
- camera motion conflicts with subject motion;
- speech and mouth movement do not align;
- the clip is visually strong but has no narrative function.

## Exercise

Plan a 15-second piece as five 3-second shots. For each shot write start frame, end frame, motion, continuity requirement and rejection condition. Generate or storyboard only after the five shots form one visual sentence.
