## Purpose

This segment demonstrates how a stored prompt library can encode previous consulting work, how research perspective changes by stakeholder level, and how an Agent output can be transformed into an executive-friendly web page or collaborative document.

## Timeline

## Stakeholder lens

| Stakeholder | Likely decision | Research emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| CEO/founder | Where the business should go | Market structure, growth, differentiation, strategic risk |
| Brand director | How the brand should develop | Positioning, annual narrative, portfolio, channel and campaign system |
| Brand manager | What to do next | Competitor activity, audience feedback, campaign and content actions |
| Product manager | What to build or improve | Use cases, complaints, unmet needs, feature and pricing evidence |

## Research prompt anatomy

1. Role and level of expertise.
2. Business decision the report must support.
3. Object, market and period.
4. Mandatory source classes.
5. Required report sections.
6. Tables and comparison dimensions.
7. Unsupported-claim and contradiction handling.
8. Final presentation format.

## Acceptance checklist

- The report answers the intended stakeholder's decision.
- Important claims have source, date and context.
- Metrics use consistent definitions.
- Competitors are comparable rather than randomly chosen.
- The executive summary matches the evidence below it.
- HTML presentation does not hide uncertainty.
- The collaborative copy appears in the correct account and document location.

## Exercise

Write three versions of the same research brief for CEO, brand director and product manager. Keep the company constant and change only the decision, questions, evidence and output. Compare how much the resulting report structure changes.
